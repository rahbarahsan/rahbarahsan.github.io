Before a team builds on a model, it now goes through one repeatable assessment. Provenance and licence checks, automated security scans, and red teaming that includes risks no public benchmark covers. What clears it lands in a catalogue teams check first.

At work · Federal public service · 2024 to now

Agents that collect the facts on every open source model

Before a model from Hugging Face or Ollama is assessed, an agentic layer fills 28 metadata fields for it, including licence, provenance, training data and known vulnerabilities. That metadata is checked against the AI policy. It decides which security checks apply and helps us set the model's risk profile and the mitigation guidance teams follow.

Assessment needs evidence before anyone looks at a model, so the agents gather it first. Agent profiles were A/B tested on a held out set, and only the best one was promoted.

Research · Carleton University · 2021 · First author

Finding rare attacks in network traffic

In the standard intrusion dataset the rarest attack class has 52 training records out of almost 126,000. I compared classical models and neural networks across resampling methods, and judged every version by weighted F1 instead of accuracy. A second paper tested conditional GAN oversampling on another dataset, and my thesis found it does not help classes this small.

Accuracy hid how badly single models did on the rare attacks, because normal traffic dominates.

At work · Federal public service · Pilot

Live subtitles in six languages

Live events needed subtitles in several languages, full of in-house acronyms that public models have never seen. A speech to translation pipeline on Whisper and GPT-4o handles it, with a terminology layer for the acronyms. It is in pilot as live subtitles.

Public models do not know our internal acronyms, so a terminology layer swaps in more than 40 of them.
